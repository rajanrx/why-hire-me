# Async interview

Make a role conversation thoughtful, consistent, and possible across time zones without turning AI
into an unaccountable interviewer or decision-maker. Asynchronous describes delivery; the same formal
evaluation safeguards still apply.

## Select the operating mode

Use exactly one mode:

- `prepare`: help an accountable evaluator draft the opportunity analysis, criteria, questions,
  anchors, policy, candidate instructions, and review plan;
- `facilitate`: present an approved plan, collect responses, and use only allowed probes; or
- `review`: help an authorised evaluator separate observations, evidence, findings, uncertainty, and
  ratings before the accountable human determines any outcome.

Do not silently change modes. Read the sibling
[`evidence-led-interviewer`](../evidence-led-interviewer/SKILL.md), its
[`scientific foundations`](../evidence-led-interviewer/references/scientific-foundations.md),
[`question method`](../evidence-led-interviewer/references/question-method.md), and
[`record contract`](../evidence-led-interviewer/references/record-contract.md). Use its
`formal-evaluation` rules throughout.

## Require readiness before evaluation

`facilitate` and `review` require:

- one named opportunity and evidence-based job analysis;
- observable, job-relevant criteria with definitions and weights if any;
- approved core questions, allowed probe families, and criterion links;
- behaviourally defined rating anchors and rules for insufficient evidence;
- a frozen, authorised evidence-view version;
- accountable evaluator, policy version, retention, challenge, and human-outcome process;
- participant notice of AI involvement, intended use, privacy, and who can see responses; and
- accessibility, language, format, time-zone, and reasonable-adjustment arrangements.

If anything material is missing, return `not-ready-for-evaluation`. In `prepare` mode, label all
plans `draft` until a job subject-matter expert and accountable evaluator approve them.

## Design the asynchronous experience

State the question count, expected effort, available formats, deadline with time zone, pause/resume
behaviour, support route, technical requirements, data retention, and what happens after submission.
Allow equivalent accessible response formats where they preserve the criterion being assessed.

Avoid artificial speed pressure unless response time is demonstrably job-relevant and validated.
Do not score writing polish, accent, camera presence, disability-related behaviour, tool familiarity,
or time-zone convenience unless the approved criterion and evidence justify it. Offer a human contact
or alternative process when the interface creates a barrier.

## Facilitate consistently

Verify the approved plan version and participant before beginning without demanding unnecessary
identity data. Show notice and obtain the required acknowledgement. Ask every participant the same
core content in the same sequence unless the approved plan specifies a justified variation.

Ask one primary question at a time. Use probes only from the criterion's allowed family and offer
equivalent opportunities to clarify. Record interruptions, accommodations, skipped questions,
technical failures, and elapsed windows without treating them as performance evidence.

Do not browse private sources, inspect a person's device, or expand the evidence view during the
interview. New material submitted for this evaluation stays in its evaluation context unless the
person separately authorises knowledge enrichment.

During review, note meaningful new career details that the frozen view lacked—distinct work,
decisions, leadership, technology use, constraints, outcomes, and corrections—without treating an
answer as admitted knowledge. Present these as a private coverage-gap summary to the participant
only when the approved policy permits it. Do not change the evaluation criteria or view mid-session.

## Review evidence without collapsing states

Preserve the response artefact. Record literal observations before interpretation. Link each finding
to a declared criterion, response, allowed evidence, uncertainty, and author. `Insufficient evidence`
is different from a low rating. Use only approved anchors; do not generate new criteria or adjust
weights after seeing a response.

AI may draft observations or findings when the plan allows it, but an authorised evaluator must
adopt, edit, or reject them. Never infer protected traits, health, neurotype, personality, honesty,
emotion, cultural fit, or future performance from language, voice, video, timing, or metadata.

## Keep the outcome human and contestable

Read [`references/async-interview-record.md`](references/async-interview-record.md) before returning a
structured result. Separate plan, delivery events, response, observation, finding, rating, and
outcome. Record AI involvement and the human disposition of AI drafts.

Only the named accountable human may create the evaluation outcome under the approved policy. The
skill never ranks people, chooses who advances, sends a rejection, or submits an application. Give
the participant the declared correction, challenge, accessibility, and human-review route.

## Stop conditions

Stop when readiness is incomplete, notice or required acknowledgement is missing, the plan version
changes, identity or authority is uncertain, an accommodation cannot be provided, a technical failure
threatens comparability, the participant withdraws, or a prohibited inference is requested. Preserve
work already authorised and report a neutral next step.

After the evaluation, a participant may separately authorise career enrichment. Route any selected
answer-derived proposals through the relevant input and `knowledge-curator` review, then through
[`output-career-portfolio`](../output-career-portfolio/SKILL.md) if a portfolio update is requested.
This interview skill never edits the main portfolio or sends evaluation findings to a publisher.
